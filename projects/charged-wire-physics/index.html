Electric Field & Electrostatic Potential

Analysis of a uniformly charged wire with linear charge density λ and length 2a

Problem Statement

Consider a straight wire of length 2a with uniform charge density \lambda (charge per unit length). The wire has negligible cross-section and lies along the x-axis from x = -a to x = a. We aim to study:

  1. The electric field vector \vec{E}(x,y) at any point in space
  2. The electrostatic potential V(x,y) at any point in space

This configuration is fundamental in electrostatics and demonstrates how continuous charge distributions generate electric fields and potentials.

Theoretical Solution

Electric Field Calculation

The electric field at point (x, y) is obtained by integrating contributions from infinitesimal wire segments. For a segment dx' at position x', the contribution is:

Electric field contribution from a segment:
\vec{dE} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{\lambda \, dx'}{r^2} \hat{r}

Breaking into components and integrating along the wire:

Electric field components:
E_x = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \int_{-a}^{a} \frac{x - x'}{[(x-x')^2 + y^2]^{3/2}} \, dx'
E_y = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \int_{-a}^{a} \frac{y}{[(x-x')^2 + y^2]^{3/2}} \, dx'

These integrals can be solved analytically. The solutions are:

Analytical solution for electric field:
E_x = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{(x-a)^2 + y^2}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{(x+a)^2 + y^2}} \right)
E_y = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{y}{|y|} \left( \frac{x+a}{\sqrt{(x+a)^2 + y^2}} - \frac{x-a}{\sqrt{(x-a)^2 + y^2}} \right) \quad (y \neq 0)

Electrostatic Potential Calculation

The electrostatic potential is a scalar quantity obtained by integrating contributions from all segments:

Potential contribution from a segment:
dV = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{\lambda \, dx'}{r}

Integrating along the wire gives:

Electrostatic potential:
V = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \int_{-a}^{a} \frac{dx'}{\sqrt{(x-x')^2 + y^2}} = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \ln \left( \frac{\sqrt{(x+a)^2 + y^2} + (x+a)}{\sqrt{(x-a)^2 + y^2} + (x-a)} \right)

The potential diverges logarithmically near the wire ends, which is characteristic of line charges.

Key Physical Insights

Symmetry Considerations

The system has symmetry about the x-axis. For points on the perpendicular bisector (x=0), the x-component of the electric field vanishes due to symmetry, leaving only the y-component. For points on the x-axis beyond the wire ends, the field resembles that of a point charge.

Behavior Near the Wire

Close to the wire (but not at the ends), the electric field approximates that of an infinite wire: |\vec{E}| \approx \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0 r}, where r is the perpendicular distance to the wire. The field is radial and perpendicular to the wire.

Behavior at Large Distances

Far from the wire (r \gg a), the system resembles a point charge Q = 2a\lambda, with field magnitude |\vec{E}| \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{2a\lambda}{r^2} and potential V \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{2a\lambda}{r}.

Field Lines and Equipotentials

Electric field lines originate from positive charges and terminate at negative charges. For a positively charged wire, field lines radiate outward. Equipotential surfaces are always perpendicular to electric field lines. Near the wire ends, both field lines and equipotentials exhibit curvature that reflects the finite length of the wire.
